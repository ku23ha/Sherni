My Story

“I began writing because speaking was sometimes too loud for what I had to say.”

Born into a culture steeped in rhythm, rain, and Rabindrasangeet, Audrita discovered early that words could hold the things hands were too small to carry.

I write because the world moves too quickly for everything to be remembered, and yet some afternoons refuse to leave.

My childhood was soundtracked by afternoon downpours against green shutters in Bengal. In Bengali, we have words that cannot be translated cleanly into English — words like অভিমান (Abhiman), the ache of hurt that can only exist between two people who love each other deeply. It was within this dual vocabulary that my poems found their home.

The Philosophy of The Quiet Garden

When I decided to build this online home, I didn’t want an algorithm-driven storefront or a portfolio designed to scream for attention. I wanted a quiet room.

That is why the first 50 writings are for myself. It is a vow of artistic integrity: to cultivate thought without an audience in mind, to let fragments stay fragments until they are ready to bloom, and to remember that writing is first a spiritual practice, and only second a publication.

“the ink does not ask to be liked.
it only asks to be honest.”

Beyond The Page

In addition to writing collections, I believe in tactile art — from hand-pressed letterpress prints to our community project, The Traveling Book, where readers across the world annotate a single physical copy and mail it to the next stranger.

Thank you for being here, for stepping softly, and for taking the time to read words that are still becoming.
